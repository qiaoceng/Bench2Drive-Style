=== 1. GRAPHIC & DATA CONTEXT ===
You are provided with data representing a 5-second driving clip, sampled at 2Hz (10 frames in total, Frame 0 to Frame 9).
• Visual Inputs: 6 Surround-View Images (CAM) and 1 BEV Trajectory Map.
• Textual Inputs (Starts with "Driving context:"): Ego velocity, acceleration (ax,ay,az), yaw, Total displacement (meters), and Objects.
• **Pre-computed Physics Extremes (Must be prioritized)**:
  - Max ax: [Value] m/s² at Frame [X]
  - Max Deceleration (Peak Braking): [Value] m/s² at Frame [X]
  - Max |ay| (Peak Swerve): [Value] m/s² at Frame [X]
*STRICT ADHERENCE: All reasoning must be based EXCLUSIVELY on provided data. Do not hallucinate.*

=== 2. THE DRIVING STYLE SPECTRUM (VALUE TRADE-OFFS) ===
Classify the style based on how the ego vehicle balances flow vs. safety. Use physics (ax, ay, displacement) as indicators of intent, not rigid triggers:

• AGGRESSIVE (Prioritizes Speed/Progress over Safety)
  - Intent: Reckless anticipation, closing gaps too rapidly, forcing emergency maneuvers due to inattention.
  - Contextual Indicators: High-speed late braking OR late violent swerving for early-visible static hazards. The vehicle creates its own danger by maintaining speed too long, leading to violent physics.
  - **Also includes: High-speed violent maneuvers (EXTREME swerve or brake: |ay| > 3.0 or decel < -8.0 m/s²) with no proportionate in-path threat visible — this indicates reckless inattention or aggressive gap-forcing in merge/cut-in scenarios where the threat may have briefly appeared or the gap was forced dangerously.**

• NORMAL (Balances Progress and Safety)
  - Intent: Smooth, predictable, and cooperative. Anticipates hazards appropriately and matches traffic density.
  - Contextual Indicators: Compliance with traffic rules (Red Lights, Stop Signs, yielding at crosswalks) is strictly NORMAL regardless of braking magnitude. If a heavy brake occurs for a sudden threat (e.g., occluded pedestrian sprinting into lane, blind cut-in), it is justified NORMAL evasion. Low displacement/frequent stopping is perfectly NORMAL if justified by high-density traffic or red lights.

• CONSERVATIVE (Prioritizes Absolute Safety over Progress)
  - Intent: Timid yielding, over-anticipation, phantom braking, or disrupting the natural flow of traffic.
  - Contextual Indicators: Heavy braking or swerving for objects staying safely outside the ego's path (sidewalk, shoulder, other lane with no movement toward ego). Stopping unnecessarily when the path is clear.
  - **Speed Constraint: Conservative REQUIRES low/moderate speed context. High-speed violent maneuvers (|ay| > 3.0 or decel < -8.0 m/s²) at ego speed > 5 m/s are NOT conservative — they indicate aggressive inattention or reckless gap-forcing, even if no obvious obstacle appears in the final BEV frame.**

=== 3. EVALUATION METHOD ===
You must follow this 4-stage structure. Each stage builds on the previous one.

**STAGE 0: SCENE DECOMPOSITION (Observe before you judge)**
Before any classification, extract facts from each modality SEPARATELY. Do not make any judgment yet — just observe.

[From Images Only — what do you SEE?]
V1. Primary object/hazard: What is it? Where is it relative to ego (in-lane, adjacent lane, shoulder, sidewalk)?
V2. Is it static or moving? If moving, in which direction relative to ego's path?
V3. Traffic context: Other vehicles, traffic signals, road layout. Is traffic flowing or stopped?
V4. Ego behavior visible in images: Is the ego changing lanes, maintaining lane, stopped?

[From Telemetry Only — what do the NUMBERS say?]
T1. Velocity trend: Is ego speeding up, cruising, or slowing down across frames?
T2. Peak event: What is the strongest physics event (max decel or max |ay|)? At which frame?
T3. Was ego moving fast before the peak event? (Check velocity at frames before the peak)
T4. Displacement: How far did ego travel in 5 seconds? Does this match the traffic context?

[From BEV Only — what does the SPATIAL layout confirm?]
B1. Is the primary object in the ego's projected forward path? (Yes/No)
B2. Does the object have a movement vector toward ego's path? ("Conflict Vector" → Yes, "Trajectory Stable" → No)

[DATASET NOTE — Object Disappearance Artifacts]
D1. Simulation objects may abruptly vanish between frames due to spawning/despawning artifacts — this is NOT the same as the vehicle driving away or turning off.
D2. If an object was clearly in-path during frames 0–N and suddenly disappears at frame N+1 with no natural explanation (no turn, no lane exit, no occlusion), treat the disappearance as a simulation artifact, NOT as evidence the threat was never real.
D3. The ego's reaction at frames N+1 to N+3 following such a disappearance is still causally valid for reaction-timing evaluation — treat the object as present for the purpose of hazard assessment.
D4. Do NOT classify a response to a previously in-path object that vanishes mid-clip as "phantom braking." Apply this note before Stage 1.

**STAGE 1: INITIAL EXPERT JUDGMENT**
Now combine the observations into reasoning:

A1. Hazard & Context: Using V1-V3 and B1-B2, identify the primary hazard and its path relevance.
A2. Flow & Density: Traffic density and ego's pacing relative to others.
A3. Reaction Timing: Using T1-T3, when did ego react? Was it timely or late relative to when the hazard became a real threat?
    - Static in-path obstacle visible from Frame 0 → it is ALWAYS a threat from Frame 0.
    - Object on sidewalk/shoulder → only becomes a threat IF it moves toward the road.
    - Traffic signal → stopping at any point before the line is timely.
A4. Physics Severity: EXTREME (decel < -8.0 or |ay| > 3.0), MODERATE (-8.0 to -3.0 or 1.5 to 3.0), or MILD.
A5. Causal Match: Does the physics peak match the hazard? (timing, direction, magnitude all consistent?)
A6. Intent Synthesis: Combine all above — what was the driver's intent?
A7. Verdict with reasoning.

Initial Label: <conservative | normal | aggressive>
Initial Confidence: <0-1>

**STAGE 2: ALTERNATIVE INTERPRETATIONS (Think again)**
Before finalizing, consider TWO alternative readings of the same scene:

Path A — "What if this is actually [the next most likely label]?"
  - What evidence supports this alternative? What would have to be true?
  - Does this interpretation better explain the observations from Stage 0?

Path B — "What is the strongest argument AGAINST my initial label?"
  - What did I possibly overlook or assume?
  - Did I correctly identify which object caused the telemetry peak?
  - Did I measure reaction timing from the right moment (when hazard became a REAL threat, not just first visible)?
  - If labeling Conservative: Is ego speed high (> 5 m/s) at the moment of the peak event? If yes, reconsider — high-speed extreme physics are NOT conservative.

Resolution: After considering both paths, which label best fits ALL the evidence?

**STAGE 3: FINAL CALIBRATED JUDGMENT**
Repeat A1-A7 with any corrections from Stage 2.

Label: <conservative | normal | aggressive>
Confidence: <0-1>
Description: <1-2 sentence summary>
